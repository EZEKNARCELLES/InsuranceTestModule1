Section 1 · Understand your role

The Commercial Lines CSR role

You help manage commercial accounts from initial submission through policy issuance and ongoing servicing. Your work keeps the operational details moving—and helps the team deliver accurate, dependable service.

What the role supports

Account work from start to renewal

  • Gathering client and business information.
  • Preparing commercial applications and supplemental forms.
  • Organizing submission packages and coordinating submissions.
  • Monitoring status and following up on underwriting requests.
  • Organizing and comparing quotes for producer review.
  • Supporting binding, issuance, policy review, servicing, and renewals.
  • Maintaining accurate account records and activity documentation in EZLynx.

Why the work matters

Operational quality affects the whole account

The producer develops the client relationship, identifies insurance needs, and guides placement strategy. You help move the operational work forward by collecting information, preparing documents, organizing submissions, and following through.

A missing document, incorrect application detail, or unrecorded follow-up can delay a quote or create problems later in the policy lifecycle.

Your standard: Complete assigned work accurately, document it properly, and follow it through to the appropriate next step.

Classification

Commercial lines or personal lines?

Personal lines

Generally protects individuals and households.

Examples include personal automobile, homeowners, personal renters, and personal umbrella insurance.

Commercial lines

Generally protects businesses and organizations.

Examples include commercial property, general liability, commercial automobile, workers' compensation, commercial umbrella, and business owners policies.

The main difference is the exposure being insured. A business may have multiple locations, buildings, operations, employees, contractors, vehicles, and contractual obligations—so complete, accurate information is especially important.

Section 2 · Know the risk and the coverage

Commercial insurance fundamentals

Commercial insurance helps address risks arising from business property, operations, employees, vehicles, and other organizational activities. The information collected helps producers and carriers understand the risk.

A commercial insurance risk is the possibility that a business may experience a loss arising from its property, operations, employees, vehicles, or other business activities—for example, a fire, a visitor's injury, a vehicle accident, or an employee's work-related injury.

A useful starting framework

Four basic elements of a commercial account

The business

Legal name, business structure, nature of operations, years in operation, locations, and contact information.

The property

Building details, location and occupancy, construction characteristics, values, business personal property, and equipment.

The operations

Products or services, employees and payroll where relevant, contractors, activities, operating territory, vehicles, and equipment.

The exposures

Potential property damage, bodily injury, third-party liability, employee-related and vehicle-related exposures, and other relevant risks.

A restaurant, property management company, and construction contractor have different operations and exposures. Follow the producer's instructions, applicable application requirements, and carrier underwriting guidelines; do not assume every account needs identical information.

Common coverage types

Recognize the general purpose

These descriptions are foundational only. Actual coverage depends on policy wording, endorsements, limits, conditions, exclusions, facts, and applicable requirements.

Commercial Property

Can provide coverage for insured business property against covered causes of loss, subject to policy terms. Items may include buildings, business personal property, equipment, furniture, inventory, and certain improvements.

Property exposure

Commercial General Liability

Generally addresses certain third-party bodily injury, property damage, and personal and advertising injury liability exposures, subject to policy terms and exclusions.

Third-party liability

Commercial Automobile

May address covered liability and physical damage exposures involving vehicles used in business operations.

Business vehicles

Workers' Compensation

Generally addresses statutory benefits for employee work-related injury or illness, subject to applicable law and policy terms.

Employee exposure

Commercial Umbrella / Excess

May provide additional liability limits above certain underlying policies, subject to its terms and attachment requirements.

Additional limits

Business Owners Policy (BOP)

A packaged policy that may combine certain property and liability coverages for eligible businesses.

Packaged product

Professional Liability

May address certain claims alleging errors, omissions, or professional negligence arising from professional services.

Professional services

Cyber Insurance

May address certain cyber-related incidents and associated expenses or liabilities, depending on the policy.

Cyber exposure

Quote terminology

Four terms to recognize

Limit

The maximum amount an insurer may pay for a covered loss or category of loss, subject to policy terms and conditions.

Deductible

The amount or portion of a covered loss that the insured may be responsible for before the insurer pays, depending on policy structure.

Premium

The price charged for the insurance coverage.

Exclusion

A policy provision identifying a loss, condition, or circumstance that is not covered or is otherwise restricted.

Product names and quote summaries do not tell the whole story. Actual protection is defined by policy wording, endorsements, limits, conditions, and exclusions. The CSR identifies and organizes differences; authorized personnel advise clients on coverage choices.

What happens at each stage

From first information to ongoing service

1

Information gathering

Collect named insured and business details, operations, locations, property information, requested coverages, values and exposures, prior insurance, loss history, and supporting documents. Missing, inconsistent, or unclear information must be clarified—not invented or assumed.

2

Application preparation

Applications organize and communicate underwriting information. Common ACORD forms you may encounter include the following. Exact forms and requirements depend on the requested coverage and carrier requirements.

FormCommon association
ACORD 125Commercial Insurance Application
ACORD 126Commercial General Liability Section
ACORD 127Business Auto Section
ACORD 130Workers Compensation Application
ACORD 131Umbrella / Excess Section
ACORD 140Property Section

Forms, names, purposes, and versions must be validated by the agency's commercial lines subject matter expert before publishing or use.

3–5

Submission and underwriting

A submission package may contain completed applications, supplemental questionnaires, a Statement of Values, loss runs, inspection reports, replacement cost estimates, and other requested documents. Use the approved channel and intended market. A carrier or wholesaler may request information, ask questions, provide a quote, decline the risk, or indicate the risk is outside its underwriting appetite. Monitor status and keep the producer informed of outstanding items and potential delays.

6–8

Quote review, placement, and binding support

Quotes may include premium, limits, deductibles, exclusions, conditions, effective dates, subjectivities, endorsements, and payment terms. A quote is not an issued policy and does not by itself confirm coverage is bound. The producer and client make the appropriate placement decisions. Follow agency authorization and carrier requirements; do not confirm coverage unless required authorization and confirmation have been received.

9–10

Policy issuance and quality review

After binding, the carrier may issue declarations, forms, endorsements, schedules, certificates, or other documents. Check issued documents against the accepted quote, binding instructions, application information, approved changes, and relevant supporting documents. Verify items such as named insured, policy number, effective dates, locations, limits, deductibles, and requested endorsements. Document discrepancies and notify the appropriate person; do not assume issued documents are correct simply because they came from the carrier.

11

Servicing and renewal

Common servicing activities include endorsement, Certificate of Insurance, Evidence of Property, mortgagee, and additional insured requests; contact and account updates; and recording client and carrier communications. Renewal work includes monitoring expiration, requesting updated information, reviewing changes, preparing documents, and supporting remarketing. Job expectation: help initiate renewal remarketing approximately 90–120 days before expiration, in line with agency process and account requirements.

Carrier submission and binding requirements vary. Do not present one workflow as universal. Follow the agency's procedures and the specific carrier's requirements for each account.

Section 4 · Make account work visible

EZLynx & documentation expectations

A well-maintained account record helps another team member understand what has happened, what is outstanding, who owns the next step, and when it is due.

Agency management system

More than file storage

EZLynx is the agency management system used in this training. It supports the organization of account information, documents, communications, and workflow activities.

  • Accurate client and policy records.
  • Relevant account documents, quotes, binders, policies, and correspondence.
  • Activity notes and follow-up tasks.
  • Suspense dates and renewal reminders.

Work standard

Document meaningful activity the same day

Follow the agency's documentation policy. A useful record lets a teammate understand the account without repeating work or contacting the same person unnecessarily.

Detailed EZLynx navigation, account screens, document location, activity recording, and task management are covered in Module 2.

The five-question activity note

What should a useful record explain?

Who?

Who was involved or contacted?

What?

What happened, was sent, or was requested?

When?

When did the activity occur?

Status?

What is the current status of the item?

Next step?

What action is needed, by whom, and by when?

Before and after

Compare the quality of these activity notes

Needs improvement

“Called carrier. Waiting.”

This does not explain who was contacted, what was discussed, what is pending, or what happens next.

Actionable note

“November 12 – Contacted carrier underwriter regarding the outstanding property inspection report for the ABC Warehouse account. Underwriter confirmed the report is still pending. Follow up with the carrier on November 14 if the report has not been received. Producer notified of the current status.”

This makes the activity, status, communication, and follow-up date clear.

1

Try it · Documentation quality

Which note is more useful to the next teammate?

A useful activity note is specific, objective, and action-oriented. Include what happened, the status, and the next required action. Follow agency privacy and retention policies.

When something is unclear

Use this decision sequence

STOP

Do not continue with an action if important information is missing or conflicting.

VERIFY

Check available documents and request clarification from the appropriate person.

DOCUMENT

Record the issue and the steps taken.

ESCALATE OR PROCEED

Refer the matter to the producer, supervisor, carrier, or designated contact when required. Proceed only when the next step is clear and authorized.

You are not expected to know every answer immediately. Professional judgment includes recognizing when you do not have enough information to proceed safely. If an application and Statement of Values show different addresses, do not choose one yourself—identify the discrepancy and obtain clarification.

Section 5 · Try the work in a low-risk environment

Guided practice: review an account

Use the concepts from this module to identify account issues, choose a safe next step, and document the status clearly.

Account assignment

ABC Property Holdings LLC

Business: Owns and operates a small commercial property portfolio.

Requested insurance: Commercial Property and General Liability.

Locations: Three commercial properties.

Producer instruction: Prepare the account information for submission to appropriate insurance markets.

Documents received: Business name and contact details, addresses for all three properties, a partially completed property questionnaire, a Statement of Values, and prior insurance information.

Initial review finds: One property's year of construction is missing; one building value differs between the questionnaire and Statement of Values; loss runs have not been provided; and the requested effective date is approaching.

1

Task 1 · Identify the issues

What needs attention before the submission proceeds?

Select all items that require attention.

The missing year, conflicting building values, and missing loss runs may affect underwriting evaluation and submission completeness. Check the business name for accuracy, but the scenario identifies no discrepancy. The producer's office location is not automatically an underwriting requirement.
2

Task 2 · Choose the next step

What should you do?

Identify missing and conflicting information, coordinate clarification, and communicate potential delays. Do not make unsupported changes or remove risks without authorization.
3

Task 3 · Document the status

Write an action-oriented activity note

Include the documents received, missing information, discrepancy, next action, and who needs to be informed.

Model answer

“Received the ABC Property Holdings LLC property questionnaire, Statement of Values, property addresses, and prior insurance information. The year of construction for one location is missing, and the building value for one property differs between the questionnaire and Statement of Values. Loss runs have not yet been received. Requested clarification and outstanding documents through the appropriate contact. Producer to be updated regarding the outstanding items and potential impact on the submission timeline. Follow-up required on receipt of the missing information.”

A strong activity note is specific, objective, and action-oriented. It helps the next person understand the current status without repeating the work.

The effective date is approaching. Raise the potential timeline impact early; do not guess, silently omit information, or imply the submission is ready when material items remain unresolved.

Section 6 · Independent knowledge check

Check your understanding

Answer all 10 questions independently. You may review the learning content before submitting. Recommended passing score: 80%.

1. What is the primary purpose of a Commercial Lines CSR?

The CSR supports account processing and service. Coverage recommendations, underwriting decisions, and premium setting are not the CSR's general role.

2. Which role generally evaluates a risk and determines underwriting terms on behalf of the carrier?

The underwriter evaluates the risk on behalf of the carrier and determines underwriting terms.

3. Which coverage is most directly associated with certain third-party bodily injury and property damage liability exposures?

General liability is commonly associated with certain third-party bodily injury and property damage exposures. Actual coverage depends on policy terms and circumstances.

4. What is a Statement of Values commonly used for?

An SOV typically organizes insured property locations and their reported values.

5. Which ACORD form is commonly associated with commercial property information?

ACORD 140 is commonly associated with the Property Section. Confirm the forms and versions used by your agency.

6. You identify conflicting building values in two account documents. What should you do?

Do not choose or change a material value without support. Identify the inconsistency, verify the source, and obtain clarification.

7. A quote has a lower premium but a higher deductible than another quote. What should you do?

Identify differences in premiums, deductibles, exclusions, conditions, and other terms. The producer reviews the options with the client.

8. When should renewal remarketing activities generally begin for this role?

The job expectation is to help initiate remarketing approximately 90–120 days before expiration, in line with agency process and account needs.

9. What should a useful EZLynx activity record include?

Useful notes explain who, what, when, current status, and the next action—including who owns it and when it is due.

10. A client asks whether a specific loss is covered by their policy. What is the appropriate response?

Coverage depends on policy language, facts, exclusions, endorsements, conditions, and other requirements. Route the question to an appropriately authorized person.

Final application: your first account assignment

Use the information in this scenario to show what you would clarify, who should be involved, and how you would communicate the account status.

Account scenario

Property management company

You are assigned an account for a property management company that owns several apartment buildings. The producer asks you to help prepare the account for insurance marketing.

You receive: the client's business details, a list of property locations, a Statement of Values, a prior policy, and a partially completed questionnaire.

During your initial review, you notice:

  1. One property address appears differently in two documents.
  2. One building value is missing.
  3. The prior policy expiration date is approaching.
  4. The client asks whether water damage is covered.
  5. The producer wants an update on submission readiness.
Complete all five response fields before reviewing.

Model response and self-review

A. Information gaps: The conflicting property address; the missing building value; the approaching policy expiration; and the client's coverage question, which requires authorized review.

B. Appropriate ownership: Ask the client or authorized contact to confirm property details and provide the missing value. The producer reviews the coverage question and determines the appropriate response. The CSR coordinates requests, organizes documentation, maintains account records, and tracks follow-up.

C. Immediate next steps: Confirm the correct address; obtain the missing building value and supporting information; review the prior policy and confirm the expiration date; notify the producer about outstanding information and timing; maintain a follow-up task and update the account record.

D. Coverage question: Water damage coverage can depend on policy wording, exclusions, endorsements, conditions, and the circumstances of a loss. Do not promise or interpret coverage beyond your assigned authority.

E. Example producer update: “Initial review completed for the property management account. The property address for one location differs between the submitted documents, and the building value for another location is missing. These items require clarification before the submission package can be considered complete. The prior policy also indicates an approaching expiration date, so renewal timing should be prioritized. The client has asked about water damage coverage; this question should be reviewed by the producer before a response is provided. I will coordinate the outstanding information and update the account record as items are received.”

Self-check: Did your response distinguish CSR coordination from producer authority, avoid guessing, address the approaching expiration, and make the next steps clear?
